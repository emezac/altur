# Complex Sales-Call Analysis — Worked Examples

The Call Analyzer earns its keep on the **hard** calls: stalled deals, gatekeepers,
competitor pressure, compliance requirements, and speakers who contradict themselves.
Anyone can summarize a clean "customer said yes" call. This document walks through how
the analyzer turns two deliberately difficult calls into structured, evidence-backed
intelligence, and why each decision is made.

The three reference calls form a **complexity ladder** (audio generated by
`input/generar_llamada_venta_{1,2,3}.py`, transcripts and analyses committed under
`backend/tests/fixtures/`):

| Call | Language / audio | Difficulty | What makes it hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 — Laura / Carlos | ES, mono | Easy (baseline) | Happy path: interested buyer, price accepted, demo booked. `positive` (0.95), `no_objections_raised`. |
| 02 — Mariana / Roberto | ES, **2-channel stereo** | Medium | Price objection + competitor + **no purchasing authority** + annoyed buyer + a dropped-line moment + dictated PII. |
| 03 — Maya / Daniel | EN, mono | Hard | Enterprise: recording consent, multi-requirement compliance (EU residency, SOC 2 Type II), budget cap vs competitor undercut, a **numeric inconsistency**, honest "I won't guess" escalation, and a "pending — not approved" negotiation. |

Everything below is the analyzer's actual output (the committed fixtures), annotated
with the transcript evidence that drives each field.

## How the analyzer models a call

Each analysis is one JSON object with two roots (see `app/workers/tasks.py` and
`app/schemas/tag_schema.py`):

- **`summary`** — narrative + graded signals: `executive_summary`, `key_points`,
  `sentiment` (`positive|neutral|negative|mixed`) with `sentiment_score`,
  `purchase_intent` (`high|medium|low`) with `intent_score`, and an `insights` block:
  `buying_signals`, `risks`, **`inconsistencies`**, `tone_notes`.
- **`tags`** — the classification layer, each from a closed enum: `outcome`,
  `next_step`, `objection`, `compliance_flag`, plus `product_interest` (open list) and
  per-tag confidences.

Two design rules matter for hard calls, and both show up below:

1. **Sentiment ≠ outcome.** A frustrated buyer can still be a strong lead; a polite one
   can be a dead end. They are scored independently.
2. **Never silently correct the numbers.** If a speaker states a figure that does not add
   up, the analyzer records it under `inconsistencies` rather than "fixing" it. This is a
   compliance and trust requirement, not a nicety (see `docs/prompt_design.md`).

## Worked example A — Call 02: the stalled deal behind a gatekeeper

**Setup.** Mariana (Altura CRM) reaches Roberto on a noisy street. Roberto is comparing
vendors, is annoyed by a previous supplier, and — critically — **cannot buy**.

### Signal → analysis mapping

| Transcript moment (turn) | Signal | Analyzer field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Esta llamada puede ser revisada con fines de calidad" (00) | recording disclosure | context for `compliance_flag` |
| "estamos comparando con Competidor Uno. Su precio parece más bajo" (03) | competitor + price pressure | `risks`, `objection` context |
| "cuatro mil novecientos sigue siendo mucho" (05) | budget objection | **`objection: price_budget`** (conf 0.95) |
| "yo no autorizo la compra. La decisión final la toma mi directora financiera" (07) | **gatekeeper / no authority** | top `risks` entry |
| "Anota un dato de prueba… correo ficticio… teléfono ficticio…" (11) | dictated personal-looking data | **`compliance_flag: possible_sensitive_data`** |
| "No necesito datos bancarios ni contraseñas" (12) | rep declines sensitive data | mitigates escalation |
| "Estoy algo molesto porque el proveedor anterior…" (13) | frustration | `tone_notes`, `sentiment: mixed` |
| dropped line → "¿Roberto, sigues en la llamada?" (16) | connection break | handled, not fatal |
| "Correcto, pero no prometo que aprobemos la compra" (19) | soft, conditional close | `outcome: follow_up_scheduled` |

### What the analyzer produced

- **sentiment: `mixed` (0.60)** — the score is deliberately mid: genuine interest coexists
  with frustration and hesitation. `tone_notes` captures the *trajectory*: "rushed and
  mildly frustrated early… improved after Mariana acknowledged concerns and proposed the
  pilot." Trajectory is what a supervisor actually needs, not a single label.
- **purchase_intent: `medium` (0.65)** — real momentum (agreed to a pilot and a follow-up)
  but capped because the buyer is not the decision-maker.
- **tags:** `outcome = follow_up_scheduled` (0.95), `next_step = send_proposal` (1.0),
  `objection = price_budget` (0.95), `compliance_flag = possible_sensitive_data`.
- **Why this is the hard part:** three distinct risks stack — *competitor at a lower price*,
  *no purchasing authority*, and *prior-vendor distrust*. A naive summarizer would log
  "customer interested, follow-up booked" and lose all three. The analyzer surfaces them as
  `risks`, so the rep walks into the next call knowing the deal hinges on the financial
  director and a competitor comparison — exactly the four items Roberto asked for (turn 09).

> **`objection` is single-valued** in the tag schema, so it records the *dominant* one
> (`price_budget`). The competitor and no-authority signals are not lost — they live in
> `risks`. That split (one headline objection tag + a full risk list) is intentional.

## Worked example B — Call 03: the enterprise deal gated by security and budget

**Setup.** Maya (SignalDesk) and Daniel run an enterprise evaluation: 18k tickets/month,
multilingual routing, hard legal requirements, a finance-imposed price cap, and a
competitor (HelpSphere) undercutting on price but missing Portuguese routing.

### Signal → analysis mapping

| Transcript moment (turn) | Signal | Analyzer field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "are you comfortable with this call being recorded…" → "Yes, that's fine, as long as payment information is not captured" | consent obtained, scope set | `compliance_flag: none` (consent handled) |
| "EU data residency, an SOC 2 Type II report, and deletion… within thirty days" | mandatory compliance reqs | `risks`, `product_interest: Security Pack` |
| "finance has capped the first year at $3,800/month" vs "standard… $4,500" | budget objection vs list price | **`objection: price_budget`** |
| "HelpSphere quoted thirty-six hundred… Can you get to thirty-seven fifty?" | competitor anchor + counter | `risks` |
| "A fifteen percent pilot discount would bring the monthly amount to $3,825… close the remaining **seventy-five dollar** gap" | **math error** ($3,825 vs $3,800 = $25) | **`inconsistencies`** |
| "please do not describe that as approved… VP of finance still has the final decision" | authority limit + wording discipline | `risks`, honesty preserved |
| "I don't want to guess" (WhatsApp media handling) | rep escalates instead of overpromising | **`buying_signals`** ("appreciated honesty") |
| "Wednesday, July 22nd… Sorry… Make that Thursday, July 23rd" | self-correction | resolved in `key_points` (Thu 23rd) |
| "If those three items check out, I will recommend moving forward" | conditional advocacy | `buying_signals`, `intent: high` |

### What the analyzer produced

- **sentiment: `mixed` (0.85)** — high score because the tone is constructive and
  professional throughout, yet still `mixed` (not `positive`) because Daniel is firm and the
  deal carries unresolved blockers. Note how **A (0.60) and B (0.85) are both `mixed`** but
  the scores separate a tense stalled call from a healthy-but-demanding one.
- **purchase_intent: `high` (0.90)** — despite more blockers than Call 02, intent is higher:
  the buyer volunteered a technical workshop and stated explicit "if X, then I recommend"
  advocacy. Again, **sentiment and intent move independently**.
- **The inconsistency catch is the headline.** The agent says "seventy-five dollar gap," but
  the pilot price ($3,825) is $25 above the $3,800 cap. The analyzer does **not** paper over
  it — it logs: *"Agent describes a 'seventy-five dollar gap' but $3,825 vs the $3,800 cap is
  a $25 difference."* That single line can stop a proposal from going out with a wrong number,
  which is precisely why the prompt forbids silent correction.
- **Honesty scored as a buying signal.** The rep refusing to guess on WhatsApp media handling
  (and Daniel explicitly appreciating it) is captured under `buying_signals` — a subtle,
  human dynamic that a keyword summarizer would miss entirely.
- **tags:** `outcome = follow_up_scheduled` (0.95), `next_step = demo_scheduled` (0.90),
  `objection = price_budget` (0.85), `compliance_flag = none`,
  `product_interest = [Enterprise Plan, Multilingual Routing, Security Pack]`.

### A note on the two compliance verdicts

Call 02 → `possible_sensitive_data`; Call 03 → `none`. Both calls mention fictional PII and
both disclose recording. The differentiator the analyzer keys on: in Call 02 the buyer
**dictates contact details aloud** (email + phone), so the transcript now *contains* data that
looks personal — worth a reviewer's eyes. In Call 03 the alias is a clearly fictional
`daniel.pilot@blueharbor.example` offered as a "test," and consent scope was set explicitly
("payment information is not captured"). The flag is about *what a human should double-check*,
not a moral judgment — and that boundary is exactly the kind of nuance a compliance reviewer
wants the tool to draw.

## Why this is valuable for the project

These two calls exercise nearly every hard path the schema exists for:

- **Multiple stacked objections** resolved into one headline `objection` tag + a full `risks`
  list (no signal dropped).
- **Gatekeepers / no purchasing authority** surfaced as a first-class risk, not buried.
- **Competitor pressure** with concrete price anchors.
- **Compliance nuance** — recording consent, data-residency/SOC 2 requirements, and a
  defensible `possible_sensitive_data` vs `none` distinction.
- **Inconsistency detection** — a real arithmetic error caught and *preserved*, not corrected.
- **Sentiment vs intent independence** — two `mixed` calls with different scores and opposite
  intent trajectories.

Together they demonstrate that the analyzer produces *decision-grade* output for the calls a
sales manager actually loses sleep over.

## Reproduce it

```bash
cd backend
# Fixtures are provider-agnostic; the fake provider replays these exact analyses.
LLM_PROVIDER=fake STT_PROVIDER=fake pytest tests/integration/test_pipeline_end_to_end.py -q

# Or run a real model over the transcripts:
#   LLM_PROVIDER=openai OPENAI_API_KEY=sk-... uvicorn app.main:app --port 8000
#   then POST the audio (input/02.wav, input/03.wav) to /api/v1/calls and open the SPA.
```

- Transcripts: `backend/tests/fixtures/transcripts/call_02.json`, `call_03.json`
- Analyses: `backend/tests/fixtures/analyses/analysis_02.json`, `analysis_03.json`
- Tag schema (allowed enum values): `backend/app/schemas/tag_schema.py`
- Prompt that enforces the "record, don't correct, inconsistencies" rule:
  `_build_analysis_system_prompt()` in `backend/app/workers/tasks.py`
